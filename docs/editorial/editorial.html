TokenRadar Editorial Flow Contract

This document defines the editorial standards and operating gates for TokenRadar content. The maintained source of record is now the generated artifact pair in docs/editorial/; the legacy EDITORIAL.md source file has been retired.

1. Core Philosophy

  • Analysts, not advisors. TokenRadar provides data, historical context, scenarios, and risk framing. It never tells users what to do with their money.
  • Objective data over hype. Avoid promotional claims, urgency, certainty, FOMO, and price-target theater.
  • Transparency. Preserve generation metadata, quality snapshots, data dates, and visible risk context wherever the content surface supports them.
  • YMYL discipline. Crypto content is financial-adjacent. Treat accuracy, caution, and source provenance as publishing requirements, not polish.

2. Canonical Publishing Flow

  1. Discovery: scripts/discover-tges.ts and narrative research identify candidates.
  2. Data harvest: scripts/fetch-crypto-data.ts, scripts/fetch-reference-articles.ts, and scripts/compute-metrics.ts refresh market data, source snippets, and TokenRadar metrics.
  3. Generation: scripts/generate-content.ts --queue writes draft article JSON into data/queue/.
  4. Queue quality gate: scripts/quality-check.ts --dir data/queue --fix --delete-failures applies blocking checks and limited auto-fixes in the daily workflow.
  5. Publication: scripts/publish-from-queue.ts injects live placeholders, rejects unresolved placeholders, builds a fresh quality snapshot, and writes approved files into content/tokens/.
  6. Post-processing: scripts/inject-internal-links.ts and scripts/repair-article-content.ts normalize links and formatting after publication.
  7. Validation and build: scripts/validate-content.ts and npm run build must pass before deploy.
  8. Delivery: committed content triggers the deploy workflow, sitemap generation, OG assets, and IndexNow where configured.

3. Source and Data Grounding

  • CoinGecko market data, TokenRadar metrics, and stored reference snippets are the primary generation context.
  • Reference snippets may guide factual grounding and style, but generated copy must not copy external article language.
  • Each article needs at least 3 specific numerical data points, excluding unresolved live placeholders.
  • Volatile market fields should use exact live placeholders during generation and be hydrated immediately before publication.
  • Missing or stale live market data blocks normal token publication. Pre-launch TGE articles may publish only with clearly marked TBA market fields.
  • TGE content must not invent tokenomics, investors, contracts, chains, exchange listings, launch dates, or official links.
  • Project descriptions from upstream feeds are source material, not approved copy. Marketing slogans, first-person claims, or promotional project language require rewrite or removal.

4. Automated Quality Gates

Blocking failures include:

  • Word count below the article-type minimum.
  • Missing ## FAQ section.
  • Missing disclaimer.
  • Fewer than 3 numeric data points.
  • Prohibited financial or promotional phrases.
  • Repeated filler paragraphs.
  • Malformed Markdown tables.
  • Unapproved outbound URLs.
  • Unresolved {{LIVE_*}} placeholders at publish time.

Warnings include borderline length, high average sentence length, unsupported heading depth, thin opening summaries, missing internal continuation links, hardcoded live-date phrasing, and disclaimer variants that do not include the standard research reminder. Warnings are not automatic blockers, but they require review for high-risk topics and important pages.

5. Required Structure and Disclosure

  • Overview and price-analysis articles fail below 800 words and target at least 1,000 words.
  • How-to-buy guides fail below 700 words and target at least 850 words.
  • TGE previews fail below 500 words and target at least 700 words.
  • All generated articles must include a useful introductory paragraph, an early summary table, ## section headings, and a ## FAQ section with 3 to 5 questions.
  • Every article should end with the exact standard disclaimer:
---
*Disclaimer: This article is for informational purposes only and does not constitute financial advice. Always do your own research (DYOR).*

The checker may accept looser disclaimer text for backward compatibility, but the editorial standard is the exact string above.

6. Prohibited and Tone-Review Language

The automated banned phrase list must cover direct advice, certainty, hype, and urgency, including:

  • "you should buy" / "you should invest"
  • "guaranteed profit" / "guaranteed returns" / "guaranteed gains"
  • "moonshot" / "to the moon"
  • "100x" / "10x" / "1000x" unless explicitly framed as a historical quote or claim being debunked
  • "buy now" / "invest now" / "act now before"
  • "sure thing" / "cannot lose" / "can't lose" / "risk-free investment"
  • "financial advice" outside the exact required disclaimer context
  • first-person recommendation language such as "I recommend buying" or "we recommend buying"

Tone review must also catch language that is not in the literal banned list: project slogans, meme copy, political framing, insider-sounding certainty, exchange availability claims without evidence, and any sentence that sounds like an issuer marketing page rather than independent analysis.

7. Manual Review and Escalation

Manual review is required when any of these are true:

  • Auto-fix rewrote prohibited language.
  • A high-risk or high-traffic article has quality warnings.
  • The article concerns an unreleased TGE, very low-liquidity token, stablecoin, tokenized security, RWA, lending/credit product, privacy asset, or how-to-buy flow.
  • Source snippets conflict, are missing, or look promotional.
  • Upstream token descriptions contain first-person, slogans, unsupported claims, or legal/regulatory assertions.
  • Publication deletes or drops queued content unexpectedly.

The reviewer checks factual claims, source provenance, tone, risk framing, legal/financial advice boundaries, rendered page structure, internal links, schema output, and the final disclaimer.

8. Failure and Remediation

  • Failed queue items should be quarantined during local/manual review and may be deleted only in the automated daily drip workflow when diagnostics are uploaded.
  • A blocked article is not silently published. Fix the root cause, regenerate or repair the article, then re-run the quality gate.
  • Unresolved placeholders, failed live data hydration, invalid JSON, malformed tables, and unapproved outbound URLs are publication blockers.
  • If a published article later fails validation or rendered QA, fix forward when narrow and revert or remove the page when the risk is broader.
  • Failure notifications should include enough context to identify token, article type, blocking issue, workflow run, and remediation path.

9. Post-Publish QA

After content reaches content/tokens/, verify:

  • scripts/validate-content.ts passes.
  • npm run build passes.
  • Rendered token pages show the intended title, FAQ, disclaimer, risk context, summary table, and internal continuation links.
  • JSON-LD, canonical URLs, sitemap entries, OG images, and IndexNow submission are consistent with the published page.
  • Telegram or workflow diagnostics are reviewed on failures.
  • Social posts that reuse the same content follow the same no-advice and no-hype rules.

10. Social Distribution Boundary

The social layer is separate from website publication, but it inherits the same editorial constraints. Hooks, captions, poll copy, video scripts, and platform-specific variants must avoid buy/sell advice, hype, unsupported venue claims, and repeated stale structures. TikTok manual fallback and Telegram reporting are operational fallbacks, not editorial approval steps.
